# Reading Philosophy

The way I often approach a new curiosity-driven project in life is by first consuming as much relevant information as I can about the subject---and it's no different with reading literature. My intention with reading great literature is to use it as a mirror for peering into my own life and mind through the lens of the greatest writers in history. 

The gift that literature (and other artistic mediums) offers is that it can indirectly illuminate human truths that reason alone can't capture. When I'm peering into the mirror of literature, implicit in my intenion is questions like, <mark><em>How is this helping me understand </em>justice<em> with more clarity?</em></mark> The same can be asked for concepts like <em>love</em>, <em>beauty</em>, <em>kindness</em>, and any other concept that only wisdom can address. 

Good literature offers us the opportunity to sit with life's most important questions---it offers us the opportunity to <em>live</em> life's most important questions in more dimensions than the single one we wake up in every morning. In this way, great literature is the opposite of escapism. Great literature---when intentionally and mindfully engaged with---helps us show up in our daily lives with greater capacities for empathy, kindness, justice, patience, forgiveness, and all the other virtues we typically attribute to a good person. 



Mary Shelley's *Frankenstein* is a book that I knew I was coming into with a lifetime of cultural expectations. The Frankenstein monster has almost become a cultural trope in my lifetime. It's interesting how much the cultural portrayal of *Frankenstein* has actually deviated from the book. I'm sure there are some commercial incentives powering that modification, but it's interesting nonetheless.
